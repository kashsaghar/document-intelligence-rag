# The Rules of Chess
### A concise reference guide

## Overview

Chess is a two-player strategy board game played on an 8x8 grid of 64 squares, alternating
between light and dark colors. Each player begins with 16 pieces: one king, one queen, two rooks,
two bishops, two knights, and eight pawns. The goal of the game is to checkmate the opponent's
king, meaning the king is under attack (in check) and has no legal move to escape capture.

## Setting Up the Board

The board is placed so that each player has a light-colored square in the bottom-right corner. The
back row (rank) for each player holds, from left to right: rook, knight, bishop, queen, king,
bishop, knight, rook. The queen always starts on a square matching her own color (white queen on a
light square, black queen on a dark square). The row in front of the back rank is filled entirely
with pawns.

## How the Pieces Move

- **King**: Moves exactly one square in any direction (horizontally, vertically, or diagonally).
- **Queen**: Moves any number of squares in a straight line — horizontally, vertically, or
  diagonally.
- **Rook**: Moves any number of squares horizontally or vertically.
- **Bishop**: Moves any number of squares diagonally. Each bishop stays on one color for the whole
  game.
- **Knight**: Moves in an "L" shape — two squares in one direction and then one square
  perpendicular to that. The knight is the only piece that can jump over other pieces.
- **Pawn**: Moves forward one square, or two squares on its very first move. It captures diagonally
  one square forward, but cannot capture or move forward if a piece blocks its path.

## Special Moves

- **Castling**: A one-time move involving the king and a rook of the same color. The king moves two
  squares toward a rook, and that rook moves to the square the king crossed. Castling is only legal
  if neither piece has moved before, there are no pieces between them, the king is not currently in
  check, and the king does not pass through or land on a square that is under attack.
- **En Passant**: If a pawn moves two squares forward from its starting position and lands beside
  an opposing pawn, that opposing pawn may capture it as though it had only moved one square
  forward. This capture must be made immediately on the very next turn, or the right to do so is
  lost.
- **Promotion**: When a pawn reaches the farthest row from its starting side, it must be promoted
  to a queen, rook, bishop, or knight of the same color, chosen by the player.

## Check, Checkmate, and Stalemate

A king is **in check** when it is under direct attack by an opposing piece. A player in check must
immediately make a move that removes the threat, either by moving the king, capturing the
attacking piece, or blocking the line of attack.

**Checkmate** occurs when a king is in check and there is no legal move available to escape the
check. When checkmate happens, the game ends immediately and the player whose king was checkmated
loses.

**Stalemate** occurs when the player to move has no legal moves available, but their king is not
in check. Stalemate results in an immediate draw.

## Other Ways a Game Can End in a Draw

- **Draw by agreement**: Both players agree to a draw.
- **Threefold repetition**: The exact same position occurs three times, with the same player to
  move each time.
- **Fifty-move rule**: Fifty consecutive moves have been made by each player without a pawn move or
  a capture.
- **Insufficient material**: Neither player has enough pieces remaining to deliver checkmate by any
  sequence of legal moves (for example, king versus king, or king and bishop versus king).

## Basic Notation

Moves are recorded using algebraic notation. Each of the 64 squares is identified by a letter
(a-h) for the file (column) and a number (1-8) for the rank (row), such as "e4" or "g7". Piece
moves are written with the piece's initial letter (K, Q, R, B, N) followed by the destination
square; pawn moves list only the destination square. A capture is marked with an "x", check with
"+", and checkmate with "#".
